p1 ... a stats dashboard in 3 days

Hive Mind Stats. A private dashboard I built with Claude Code for Facebook, Instagram, YouTube and both of our websites.

Next.jsTypeScriptTailwindSupabaseVercelMeta Graph APIGitHub Actions

Why I built it

Checking how Hive Mind was doing meant opening five dashboards. Each one named its metrics differently and reported with a different delay, and comments and messages sat in separate apps. I wanted one page that updates itself and shows every channel in a minute.

What it does

  • Overview: a card for each account with followers, the latest full day (dated, because platforms report late), 7 and 30 day totals and a chart.
  • Account pages: daily charts for new followers, reach, views, engagement and profile visits, plus a table of recent posts or videos.
  • Websites: Google Analytics 4 for both domains, the most viewed pages and a table of every blog post with views, readers, average reading time and the share of readers who reached the end.
  • Inbox: comments from all three platforms and messages from Messenger and Instagram in one list, newest first, with a link to the original.
  • PDF report for any period, compared with the previous period of the same length.
  • Automatic sync every 4 hours, with a status badge on each connection.

How it works

Next.js 16 with TypeScript and Tailwind on Vercel, with Supabase for data and login. Sign-in is an email link for allowlisted addresses only, every table has row level security, and platform tokens are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, so even a logged-in user never sees them.

Data comes from the Meta Graph API, the YouTube Data and Analytics APIs and the Google Analytics Data API. GitHub Actions runs the sync, because the free Vercel plan allows a scheduled job only once a day.

Problems I solved

  • Meta retires metrics regularly, so each metric is requested separately. When one disappears, the rest still sync and the dashboard shows what is missing.
  • Platforms do not return past follower counts, so the dashboard saves a daily snapshot and calculates growth from the differences.
  • Grouping the Instagram metrics into one request per day cut the first sync from about 200 API requests to about 40.
  • Blog posts are recognised by the date in their address, which works on both sites without manual setup.

Limits

Platform data arrives one to three days late. Seven day reach is a sum of daily values, so one person can be counted more than once. Facebook hides some commenters’ names, and comments are collected from the 25 most recent posts.

How it was made

I built it in three days with Claude Code. I wrote the requirements, set up the services (Meta, Google Cloud, Supabase, Vercel) and tested everything on the real accounts. The AI assistant wrote the code.

p2 ... 227 honey plants

The Encyclopedia of Honey Plants on hivemindapp.space.

HTMLCSSJavaScriptWikimedia APIWordPress

What it is

An interactive encyclopedia of bee forage plants for the Hive Mind site. It covers 227 plants found in Poland, 51 of them trees, from hazel in February to ivy in October. Each card shows when the plant blooms, whether it gives nectar, pollen or honeydew, where it grows, what it means for an apiary and what honey it makes.

How people search

You can type a Polish or Latin name, a month, a season, a habitat or a pollen colour, and the list filters as you type. Search ignores Polish diacritics and understands English words such as autumn or honeydew.

Quick filters narrow the list to trees, field crops, plants in bloom this month, honeydew sources or plants worth growing in a garden next to the hives. A 12 month bar on every card shows the bloom period at a glance.

Where it meets the product

A tag marks the genera that the Hive Mind model recognises from satellite imagery, with a plain note that results are probabilities and the data runs about a month behind. Pollen colour appears on 90 cards, and only 21 of them are shown as confirmed. Published sources disagree on the rest, so those are labelled approximate.

Accuracy

The plant list was checked against Dr Aneta Sulborska-Różycka’s index of honey plants and against nectar yield research from the Research Institute of Horticulture in Puławy, plus regional advisory centre materials. Two review rounds corrected 30 claims, and plants the sources did not support were left out.

Built with

HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript in a single file with no dependencies, the Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons APIs for photos with author and licence credits, WordPress and Elementor. Photos load as the reader scrolls. Made with an AI assistant.
